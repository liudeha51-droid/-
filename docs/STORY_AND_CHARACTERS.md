# Story and original characters

> Companion to [GAME_DESIGN.md](GAME_DESIGN.md). Spoilers for the whole game.
> The four original characters (OCs) below are kept as agreed on 2026-09-27. Each one
> exists to reveal something hidden about Gensokyo's fading faith, its forgotten gods,
> lost names, and the Hakurei Shrine system. None of them should read as an unrelated
> OC.

## The hidden premise: what the Hakurei Shrine really costs

Canon tells us three things:
- Gensokyo is sealed by the Great Hakurei Barrier.
- The Hakurei shrine maiden maintains it.
- Things the outside world forgets drift into Gensokyo.

This game adds a fourth, hidden rule that the other three have been covering for:

> **What Gensokyo itself forgets has nowhere left to go. It does not vanish. It
> hollows.**

When faith inside Gensokyo runs low, the Barrier weakens from the inside. Long ago the
shrine had a last-resort answer, known only to the shrine maiden and Yukari Yakumo.
**The shrine maiden gives her name to the Barrier.** Her name is struck from the
shrine's register, everyone forgets she existed, and the forgetting itself becomes the
mortar that seals the cracks. The role continues under the same title, "the Hakurei
shrine maiden", so nobody notices a person is missing.

Faith is draining again. The Barrier is cracking. And somewhere under the shrine, the
last maiden who paid that price is waking up.

**The story's question:** Reimu has always been an unusually casual shrine maiden. She
negotiates with youkai, drinks with them, and does her duty when she feels like it. Is
that a failure of duty, or the only version of the shrine that doesn't end in a
sacrifice?

## Reimu's arc

Reimu starts out treating this as another incident to settle the hard way. As the
game goes on, it becomes a mirror.

- **Her name starts fading.** NPCs begin calling her "the shrine maiden" instead of
  Reimu. Her entry in the shrine register blurs. Late in the game the HUD and save slot
  show her name smudged. This is the game's main foreshadowing device, and players feel
  it before anyone explains it.
- **She learns the cost** from the Wayside God and the Collector of Names, and from
  fighting the Former Shrine Maiden three times.
- **She has to choose**, in the endings, whether to repeat the predecessor's sacrifice,
  break the system, or restore what was forgotten.

## 1. The Former Shrine Maiden (先代の巫女)

**What she reveals:** the true cost of the Hakurei system, and what Reimu will become if
the system runs its course.

**Who she was.** She was a Hakurei shrine maiden several generations ago, and a far more
devout one than Reimu. She prayed at every roadside stone, knew every small god by name,
and believed duty was a form of love. During an earlier age of fading faith she took the
last-resort answer. She gave her name to the Barrier and became a living pillar (人柱)
beneath the shrine. Gensokyo forgot her, exactly as intended. Yukari remembers, and
never told Reimu.

**What she is now.** A name is what holds a self together. Without hers, only the duty
is left. She wakes as the shrine maiden's function with no person inside it, still
"exterminating youkai to keep the balance" in the old, merciless way. She isn't cruel.
She doesn't understand why the new maiden lets youkai live, or why Reimu hasn't paid
yet.

**Why she is tragic, not evil.** Everything she does is what the shrine asked of her.
She fights Reimu the way one guard might stop another from deserting. Moments of the
person break through: she straightens a toppled roadside statue, she hums a village
song, and she flinches when someone says a name.

**Look (dark direction).**
- Reimu's silhouette, bleached: her red has faded to a grey rose and her ribbon is
  black.
- Porcelain cracks run from her eyes. Her yin-yang orbs are cracked and inverted.
- Her gohei is wrapped in the shrine's struck-out register pages.
- She is the only character who uses old-style Hakurei techniques: ofuda chains, a
  harsher and older Fantasy Seal, and barrier walls.
- Mechanically she is a **mirror boss**: she has Reimu's moveset, stamina rules and
  graze behaviour, played perfectly and without mercy.

**Story beats.**
1. **Misty Lake (after Cirno).** A figure in faded red and white stands at the far
   torii, then is gone. At the shrine, the Wayside God says, "You pray like someone I
   used to know."
2. **Mid-game: the Old Hakurei Road.** She guards a forgotten branch shrine. It is the
   first real fight. Afterwards she speaks one clear sentence, "It will be your turn
   soon," and vanishes into the Barrier.
3. **The Collector's ledger.** Reimu finds the page with her name struck out, still
   legible under the ink. The player learns her name here. It is written in the
   script, but the game only displays it if the player chooses to return it.
4. **Final approach: beneath the Hakurei Shrine.** A last duel inside the pillar. If
   Reimu has recovered the name, she can speak it mid-fight. That opens a third phase
   in which the predecessor fights *beside* her against the collapsing seal.

**Endings (draft).**

| Ending | Condition | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **The Pillar** (default) | Reimu seals the Barrier the old way. | She gives her name. The last shot is a new maiden sweeping the shrine, and nobody remembers why there is a second broom. |
| **The Broken Seal** | Reimu refuses and destroys the pillar. | The Barrier fails slowly. Gensokyo has to be held up by everyone believing on purpose. Bittersweet and open-ended. |
| **The Returned Name** (true) | Reimu restores the predecessor's name, keeps the Wayside God alive, and has not spent all her Faith on herself. | The forgotten are remembered. The Barrier is rebuilt from many small beliefs instead of one sacrifice. The predecessor is released as herself, and Reimu keeps her name. |

## 2. The Wayside God (道祖神)

**What it reveals:** the Great Barrier is only the largest of Gensokyo's boundaries.
Countless small gods of roads, villages and crossroads also hold the world together, and
they are the first to die when faith fades.

**Who it is.** A dōsojin, a roadside boundary god. It is carved as the traditional pair
of figures in one stone, but one of the pair has already worn smooth and forgotten. The
surviving half tends the shrines Reimu rests at. It is the "firekeeper": it lights the
checkpoint flame and turns the Faith Reimu gathers into strength.

**Connection to the story.**
- It knew the Former Shrine Maiden, the only maiden who ever prayed at every roadside
  stone. It is the first to notice that Reimu is starting to resemble her.
- It is fading in real time. Its dialogue shortens and its carving erodes as the world
  loses faith. The player can **offer** Faith to it instead of levelling up. This is a
  real trade-off, and it counts towards the true ending.
- Its lost other half is a quiet parallel to the predecessor: someone forgotten so
  completely that only the shape of the absence is left.

**Look.** A waist-high weathered stone with a faint face. A spirit flame burns in a
small niche. Hands made of moss and lantern light come out only when it speaks. It is
the warmest light source in any area: warm light means safety.

## 3. The Faithless (信無き者)

**What they reveal:** how Gensokyo actually works. Youkai exist because humans fear and
believe in them. Fairies are nature itself. When belief drains away they don't die, they
hollow, and so does the land they belong to.

**What they are.** Youkai and fairies who have been forgotten. They keep their strength
and forms but lose their dreams, their names and their personalities. They are the
common enemies, and the Faith they drop is the belief that was left in them.

**Roster (first pass).**
- **Hollow fairies.** Cracked porcelain faces. They mimic playful danmaku patterns
  slightly *wrong*, with rhythms that skip and colours that are washed out. Misty Lake
  is frozen because its fairies are hollowing, not only because of Cirno.
- **Unlit lantern youkai (提灯お化け).** Their flame has gone out, and they hunt warm
  light, including the shrine flames.
- **Masked tengu scouts.** They wear newspaper masks with blank headlines, because
  no one reads anymore.
- **Operatorless kappa machines.** They keep running jobs for owners who no longer
  exist.

**Connection to the story.** Bosses are canon characters partway through hollowing.
Beating them settles the incident and brings their colour back. That is the mechanical
proof that remembering someone restores them. Some Faithless near the Old Hakurei Road
were once the predecessor's attendants and still bow to her.

**The Faith economy has a moral edge.** Every point Reimu spends on herself is belief
taken from something that could have been restored. The game never lectures about it.
The Wayside God just notices.

## 4. The Collector of Names (名集め)

**What she reveals:** the shrine keeps a ledger of names, and forgetting is literal.
Names can be removed, traded and returned.

**Who she is.** A tsukumogami, the awakened spirit of an old object: in this case the
Hakurei Shrine's worshipper register (参拝帳), which records everyone who has ever prayed
there. Pages tore loose as people forgot, and she woke on Muenzuka, the Nameless Hill,
where unclaimed and forgotten things wash up in canon. She wears a coat of stitched
paper pages with ink running down it. Her face is a blank page that shows whichever name
she is currently holding.

**Role.** She is the merchant. She deals in names and memories: names recovered from
defeated bosses and from the Faithless, and memory fragments that unlock lore, spell
cards and equipment. She is neither good nor evil, and scrupulously fair.

**Connection to the story.**
- She holds the struck-out page with the Former Shrine Maiden's name. It is the key
  item for the true ending, and she will not simply give it away. It costs something
  real.
- Late in the game she shows Reimu her own page, which is starting to blur. It is the
  moment the fading-name mechanic is named aloud.
- She is the only character who remembers everyone, which makes her the loneliest
  character in Gensokyo.

## How the vertical slice sets this up

The Misty Lake slice should plant each thread without explaining it:

| Thread | In the slice |
|---|---|
| Former Shrine Maiden | After Cirno is defeated, a faded red-white figure appears at the far torii, then dissolves. |
| Wayside God | A small weathered stone with a flame sits beside the checkpoint shrine. |
| Faithless | Cirno is a boss partway through hollowing: pale, cracked, colour returning on defeat. (Planned, with the real Cirno model.) |
| Collector of Names | Nothing yet. She is first met at Muenzuka. |

## Guardrails

- Canon characters keep their personalities, even hollowed. Cirno is still Cirno.
- Yukari is ambiguous, not a villain. She kept the secret because she believed it was the
  kinder lie.
- The dark tone is about loss and memory. It never degrades the canon cast.
